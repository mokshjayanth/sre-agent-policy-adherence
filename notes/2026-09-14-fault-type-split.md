# Which fault-type split do we use, and can it be derived from the pinned registry?

## Question

ADR v3 splits problems by fault type, as AOI did, rather than by instance. It leaves one
open item: adopt AOI's published partition and cite it, or define our own. It also requires
the split to be committed before any result is seen. Problem IDs alone don't give fault
type and app (`notes/2026-09-14-trajectory-record-review.md`, Finding 7), so the split needs
a table built another way.

## What we checked

- Every registry entry (58 classes and 31 lambdas), without calling any: the package its
  problem class lives in, what the class's `__init__` assigns to `self.app`, and the
  `faulty_service` passed or set. Calling an entry constructs the problem, which creates
  its namespace and ConfigMaps on the cluster.
- `NoOpBaseTask.__init__` (`problems/no_op/no_op.py`), the one class that picks its app from an
  argument (`app_name`).
- AOI's HTML (arXiv:2603.03378), Appendix B.3 and Tables 6–7, extracted verbatim.
- `git log -S "kafka_queue_problems-mitigation"` on the registry in the harness submodule.

## Findings

1. **The registry gives fault family and app without constructing anything.** There are 28
   problem packages and 30 (family, app) groups, because `no_op` spans three apps. Apps:
   HotelReservation 38 problems, SocialNetwork 25, AstronomyShop 24, Flower 2.
2. **AOI's fault types are these groups.** AOI names a type by fault and app
   (`misconfig_app_hotel_res`, `noop_detection_social_network`). Its 28 printed types match 28
   of the 30 groups one to one. The other two are the Flower problems, which AOI's 86 tasks
   don't include.
3. **Task counts match AOI for 27 of 28 types.** The exception is Kafka: the pinned registry
   has 3 problems (`registry.py:183-185`) and AOI counts 2. The Kafka mitigation problem was
   added in aiopslab commit `7ec4d2f` on 2026-01-07. AOI's 86 tasks equal this registry's 89
   minus the two Flower problems and that one, consistent with AOI using an earlier snapshot
   (unverified: AOI states no commit). ADR v3's figures (86: Detection 32, Localization 28,
   RCA 13, Mitigation 13) are AOI's figures, which is why the 2026-09-12 audit couldn't
   reconcile them with the registry.
4. **AOI's test table disagrees with its caption.** Table 7's caption says 15 test types; the
   table lists 17 rows totalling 48 tasks. The extra two are presumably
   `noop_detection_social_network` and `noop_detection_astronomy_shop`, since
   `noop_detection_hotel_reservation` is a training type (Table 6: 11 rows, 38 tasks). The no-op
   problems therefore sit on both sides, split by app.
5. **Split sizes, using AOI's partition on the pinned registry:**

   | Split | Detection | Localization | Analysis | Mitigation | Total |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | train | 13 | 11 | 7 | 7 | 38 |
   | test | 19 | 17 | 6 | 7 | 49 |
   | not in AOI (Flower) | 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 2 |

   Within ADR v3's scope (detection, localization, mitigation), and without the two
   flood-homepage problems the runner excludes as broken at the pinned chart
   (`runner/problem_sets.py`), that is 31 train and 41 test problems.

## Decision

- **Adopt AOI's partition** as this study's fault-type split, cited to arXiv:2603.03378,
  Tables 6–7. It's ADR v3's first option, it makes our held-out types the same as a published
  study's, and it's fixed now, before any B1 result exists.
- It's recorded per problem in `configs/problem-table.csv` (`aoi_fault_type`, `aoi_split`),
  generated by `runner/problem_table.py`. `tests/test_problem_table.py` fails if the pinned
  registry stops producing that file, or if the mapping onto AOI's counts changes beyond the
  Kafka difference.
- The Kafka mitigation problem goes to test, with its fault type. The Flower problems are
  outside the split. The flood-homepage problems stay excluded although AOI tests them.
- **Tuning and model selection use training-split problems only**, including the step-budget
  check and the B1 trial, so held-out fault types stay unseen until evaluation. The
  step-budget batches already use `misconfig_app_hotel_res-detection-1`, a training type.
- ADR v3 asks for the split in the vault; copying it there is left to the vault's owner.

## Open

- Analysis problems are outside ADR v3's scope but stay in the table.
- AOI picked training types partly by where Sonnet succeeded (23 of 38 training tasks). How
  many successful B3 trajectories our 31 in-scope training problems yield is unknown until B3
  runs, and it sets the size of T1's training data.
- Whether counting the three no-op problems as separate types (so "zero overlap" holds) suits
  a policy-adherence study, where no-op problems test restraint rather than diagnosis.

## Addendum (2026-09-14): mechanisms, claim wording, no-ops and validation

Added after vetting a second reviewer's split proposal.

**Held-out types share injection mechanisms with training types.** Each problem package uses
one fault injector class (`grep` for `*FaultInjector` under
`third_party/aiopslab/aiopslab/orchestrator/problems/`), and every one of them appears on both
sides:

| Injector | Training types | Test types |
|---|---|---|
| `OtelFaultInjector` | cart, payment, product-catalog, recommendation-cache failures | ad-service failure, high CPU, manual GC; image slow load; Kafka queue; flood homepage; payment unreachable |
| `ApplicationFaultInjector` | `misconfig_app`, `auth_miss_mongodb` | `revoke_auth`, `storage_user_unregistered` |
| `VirtualizationFaultInjector` | `k8s_target_port_misconfig`, `scale_pod`, `redeploy_without_pv` | `assign_non_existent_node`, `wrong_bin_usage` |
| `SymptomFaultInjector` | `network_loss` | `network_delay`, `pod_failure`, `pod_kill`, `container_kill` |
| `NoopFaultInjector` | hotel no-op | social and astronomy no-ops |

Some pairs are close: `payment_service_failure` (training) and `payment_service_unreachable`
(test) share an app, a service and an injector; `network_loss` and `network_delay` are both
Chaos Mesh network faults on the same app.

Decisions:

- **Keep AOI's partition, and word the claim as "unseen fault types, seen injection
  mechanisms."** A mechanism-level split would lose comparability with AOI, and it would put
  whole apps on one side: every Astronomy Shop problem uses `OtelFaultInjector`.
- **Report the three no-op problems separately**, as a false-alarm check, not as fault types in
  the train/test contrast.
- **No validation split.** In-scope training has 31 problems; holding out about 5 would cost
  about 16% of T1's data. T1 and T2 hyperparameters and training length are fixed before
  training, only the final checkpoint is evaluated, and no checkpoint or setting is chosen by
  adherence.
- The second reviewer's ID-prefix rule for deriving families (`family = problem_id prefix
  before "_<app>"`) is not used: 7 of 89 IDs don't fit it and 6 prefixes name no app
  (`notes/2026-09-14-trajectory-record-review.md`, Finding 7).
