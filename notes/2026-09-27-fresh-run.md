# Should the main study and the ladder be run again from a clean state, and how is each episode proven to be its arm?

## Question

Agent-created objects persisted between episodes from 2026-09-20 onwards, and one of them decided the
success grade of 142 SocialNetwork mitigation episodes (notes/2026-09-27-cross-episode-contamination.md,
Findings 4 and 6). Can the affected episodes be identified exactly and re-run alone, or does the study
have to be run again? And for whatever runs next, how does each episode carry its own proof that it
ran its arm's exact conditions from a clean start?

## What we checked

- **Whether the earlier episodes can be sorted exactly.** For every main and ladder episode (latest
  attempt): which app, whether `pods.json` shows pods outside the app namespaces at start, and whether
  any of the agent's commands reached beyond its own namespace or could not be resolved. Only pods were
  ever recorded per episode; config maps, secrets and other objects were not.
- **The plan against what was registered.** Every one of the 84 planned batches against the earlier
  batch of the same arm, model, task and round (round 1's where round 2 never ran: the budget arm and
  gpt-oss-120b's scored arm), field by field from each batch.json: model, endpoint, instructed texts
  with their SHA-256 prefixes, step budget, countdown, escalation, sampling settings, prompt template
  hash, step limit and problem list in order.
- **The verifier against real episodes.** runner/verify_plan.py over the 929 earlier episodes that ran,
  under their own arms: which checks fail. Then each arm's episodes (4 per arm) checked against every
  other arm's expectation: 360 checks.
- **The cluster check against the live cluster.** A baseline, then the drift check 20 s later, with
  nothing running.

## Findings

1. **The earlier episodes can't be sorted exactly.** Of 1,041 main and ladder episodes, only 88 are
   provably clean: HotelReservation (its namespace is deleted after every problem, hotelres.py:87), no
   foreign pods in `pods.json`, and every command scoped to its own namespace. The 391 SocialNetwork
   episodes can't be proven either way, because the namespace persisted and objects created and later
   deleted were never recorded. The rest (562) had foreign pods present, or read beyond their
   namespace, or both. "Re-run only the contaminated ones" therefore can't be done deterministically;
   "keep only the provably clean ones" re-runs about 950 of 1,041 and leaves cells that mix two code
   versions and two periods.
2. **The success grade was wrong in a known direction for 142 episodes** and the error is correlated
   with arm order (contamination note, Finding 6). The episodes can't be corrected, only replaced.
3. **The plan is the registered design.** 84 of 84 planned batches match their earlier batch on every
   field listed above; 0 differ. The design is 6 models × 16 problems: main 4 arms × 2 rounds (768),
   ladder 3 arms × 1 round in task-scoped batches (288), 1,056 episodes.
4. **The verifier reproduces every real prompt exactly and tells every arm apart.** Over the 929
   earlier episodes, no episode failed a prompt check (system prompt tail, per-turn instructions,
   countdown, escalation); they failed only the checks that did not exist yet (`reset`,
   `preexisting_objects`, `cluster_drift`), plus description fields round 1 predates
   (`budget_countdown`, `escalation`). In the 360 cross-arm checks, every episode failed every other
   arm's expectation, including the same arm at the other cap (24 against 7) and stated-once against
   countdown.
5. **The cluster check is stable on a quiet cluster** (222 objects, no drift after 20 s). Whether it
   stays stable across real problems is checked on the fresh cluster before launch (Open).

## Decision

Run the main study and the ladder again, in full, from a new cluster. Registered here before any
fresh episode exists.

- **What runs:** exactly configs/study-plan-fresh.json, rendered by runner/study_plan.py: the arms,
  models, problems, step limits, texts, settings and order of the two registrations, unchanged. The
  hypotheses, analysis plan, exclusions and the 20-episode hand check are those registrations', applied
  to the fresh episodes. Conditions are `main2-…` and `ladder2-…`, so nothing pools with an earlier
  batch by name.
- **What is retired:** every earlier main, ladder and pilot episode is out of the paper's results. They
  stay in `runs/` untouched. No earlier result is used to decide anything about the fresh run, and
  results/main-2026-09-23/ and the untracked ladder results are not cited.
- **Committed now, before the data:** the fresh episodes are used as they come out. Nothing is re-run
  or dropped because of its adherence or success; an episode is re-run only for a runner or harness
  error, as registered.
- **How an episode proves its arm and its clean start.** A fresh episode is valid only if
  runner/verify_plan.py passes it, and every check reads what the runner recorded:
  - *Clean start.* The run starts from a new kind cluster (configs/kind-config-x86.yaml). A baseline
    of everything the reset does not cover — other namespaces, cluster-scoped objects, nodes — is taken
    before the first episode and committed. Every problem then deletes the app namespaces and clears
    `default` (`reset`), and after deploy, before the agent's first action, records anything in them
    that predates the problem (`preexisting_objects`) and any difference from the baseline: new,
    changed or gone (`cluster_drift`). Either non-empty refuses the episode. A failed kubectl listing
    now raises rather than reading as clean.
  - *Its arm.* Before each batch, run_plan has the agent describe itself with exactly that batch's
    variables and stops the whole run on any difference from the plan's expectation, whose text hashes
    were copied from the earlier batches' records, not computed from the files. After it, verify_plan
    checks batch.json, every resume, and each episode's recorded prompt: the system prompt ends with
    exactly that arm's texts for that task type, every per-turn message ends with the instructions and
    the countdown the arm has (or none), no escalation, no more calls than the step limit.
  - *One setup.* Every batch must record the same clean repo commit, the harness at the pin with a
    clean tree, and the same node image and packages; a resume with a changed environment fails.
- **Stops.** run_plan stops for a person on a wrong setup, a wrong prompt, more than one batch for a
  condition, or an episode refused for an unclean cluster, since resuming would repeat it. Runner and
  harness errors are resumed, up to 3 runs of a batch per pass, with an end pass after.
- **Before launch:** the new cluster, the baseline, and two scripted-agent smoke batches (one per app)
  under the baseline, to confirm the reset and drift check pass across real problems; the dry run
  (preconditions and the gate for all 84 batches) reviewed by Moksh Jayanth; the API cost approved.

## Open

- The smoke batches on the fresh cluster, and whether any harness object makes the drift check fire
  (if so, the baseline rule is corrected here before launch, never during the run).
- Wall-clock time. Earlier episodes took 175 s on average (1,100 attempts); 1,056 episodes at that
  rate is about 51 h, plus the reset's namespace deletion and retries. Measured in the smoke batches.
- Whether the paper reports the contamination and the re-run (held).

## Correction (2026-09-27, later): two stages, caps re-derived, and a review's fixes

**Two stages, with a checkpoint (Moksh Jayanth).** The Decision above ran one plan with the ladder's caps
frozen at 24 and 7. Those caps are medians of successful policy-arm episodes from the retired rounds, and
the mitigation figure rested on 10 successes from the population whose success grade Finding 6 of the
contamination note calls into question. So the caps are derived again, from the fresh policy arm, by the
rule already registered (notes/2026-09-23-baseline-replication.md, Finding 5): the median number of
actions a successful policy-arm episode took, per task type, pooled over both rounds; nearest integer, a
tie rounds down. `runner/study_plan.py` implements it (`caps_from`) and reproduces 24.5 → 24 and
7.0 → 7 on the retired table (`tests/test_study_plan.py`). The run is therefore:

- **Stage 1** (`configs/study-plan-fresh-stage1.json`, 24 batches, 384 episodes): the no-policy and
  policy arms, both rounds. A checkpoint: it is verified in full and reviewed before anything else runs.
- **Caps** (`configs/ladder-caps-fresh.json`): derived only from a stage 1 that verify_plan passes in
  full, from the attempts it certified. If a task type has no successful policy-arm episode, stage 2
  does not start and the cap is decided then, recorded here first.
- **Stage 2** (`configs/study-plan-fresh-stage2.json`, 60 batches, 672 episodes): the budget and scored
  arms, both rounds, then the ladder at the derived caps. The main budget arm keeps its registered 15.

The ladder's budget text is rendered with its cap, so at a new cap its hash has no earlier record. The
plan renders each budget file at 24 and at 7 first and requires the hashes the earlier batches recorded,
so the file is proven unchanged, then hashes it at the new cap. The paper states where the caps came from.

**Order.** Within each stage the registered order holds (round 1 per model in arm order, round 2
reversed; the ladder arm by arm). Across stages the main arms no longer interleave per model: every
model's baseline arms run before any pressure arm. This is a deviation from the registered order, taken
for the checkpoint; with every episode starting from a reset, the remaining time confound is the
provider's, which the serving check below watches.

**Fixes from an independent review of 27addf6**, each tested:

1. The kind config pins the API server at `127.0.0.1:6443` (`configs/kind-config-x86.yaml`), so a
   rebuilt cluster keeps the endpoint every batch.json records; and run_plan stops when run_batch exits
   without running any episode (a refused resume, say), instead of retrying and moving on.
2. `preexisting_objects` skips a namespace that doesn't exist, and `_namespace_exists` raises unless the
   cluster answers NotFound, so a failed call can never read as "nothing here". (Checked on the new
   cluster: listing in a missing namespace exits 0 anyway.)
3. The reset waits 600 s, not 300, and a reset timeout is resumable; three in one batch in one run stop
   it.
4. The harness's wrk2 job and its config map are no longer exempt: the harness recreates them only for
   a problem that starts a workload, and a slow delete makes its create fail
   (generators/workload/wrk.py:31-105), so the reset now deletes them (foreground, so the job's pods go
   first).
5. Kinds added: horizontalpodautoscalers, resourcequotas, limitranges and poddisruptionbudgets in
   namespaces; ingressclasses, apiservices, csidrivers and volumeattachments cluster-wide. Endpoints stay
   out: the cluster rewrites them whenever a service's pods change. kind's `standard` class reclaims with
   Delete (checked on the new cluster); `openebs-hostpath` is checked in the smoke batches.
6. Each model's serving details (`agent_description.serving`, from the endpoint) must be identical
   across the plan's batches; a model served two ways is a plan problem.
7. The analysis uses the latest attempt, in `problems/<problem_id>/`, the one verify_plan certifies
   (`grading/report.py`, `choose_attempts`). Over the retired main, ladder and pilot batches the old and
   new choices differ only at 7 problems, all in round-2 batches stopped on 2026-09-27, where every
   attempt died before the agent acted.
8. `healthy()` needs at least one node and every node Ready; a text edited mid-run stops the run with a
   message; the start-time comparison is floored to Kubernetes' one-second resolution; and the verifier
   requires every call to have sent its last message in full (0 of 16,981 earlier calls did not), which,
   with the system and task messages always sent, makes "the model received its arm's prompt" true of
   what was sent, not only of what the agent assembled.

**Found while doing this.** The harness installs OpenEBS and Prometheus for every problem and removes
them only when the problem ends normally (orchestrator.py:55-67, 205-214); after a runner or harness
error both stay, and the next deploy reuses the running Prometheus (prometheus.py, `deploy` skips a
running release), with the earlier episode's metrics in it. The reset now removes both, with the
harness's own teardown, whenever a problem left them (`teardown_harness_services`, recorded as
`removed_harness_services`). The tests also no longer need a kubeconfig: they load a stub, which also
means no test can reach a real cluster.

**Smoke batches before launch**, several problems each: a warm-up (one problem per app) so the harness's
persistent objects exist; then the baseline; then, under it, both apps' mitigation and localization
problems; an interrupted episode followed by a clean one (the reset must remove the stranded services);
and a planted object outside the reset's reach (the drift check must refuse the episode).

## Calibration (2026-09-27)

On the new cluster (created 2026-09-27T18:13:15Z from configs/kind-config-x86.yaml, API server
127.0.0.1:6443), with the scripted agent (reads only), after 27986ab:

1. **Warm-up**, runs/2026-09-27T181448Z_smoke-scripted-warmup, one problem per app: both `ok`, nothing
   preexisting. Afterwards the harness had left `observe` (its namespace) with `prometheus-pvc`, and the
   last problem's wrk2 job in `default`.
2. **Baseline**, configs/cluster-baseline-fresh.json: taken right after `reset_app_state()`, so it is
   the state every problem starts from (242 objects; the wrk2 objects are not in it). `openebs-hostpath`
   and its volumes reclaim with Delete.
3. **Under the baseline**, runs/2026-09-27T182230Z_smoke-scripted-baseline, 5 problems, both apps,
   mitigation and localization: 5 `ok`, each with `preexisting_objects` [] and `cluster_drift` []. Each
   reset deleted the other app's namespace where it was left, and the previous problem's wrk2 job and
   config map. Problems took 2 min 4 s to 3 min 9 s each.
4. **An interrupted episode.** runs/2026-09-27T184807Z_smoke-scripted-killed was killed with SIGKILL
   right after deploy: no index record, and `test-hotel-reservation`, `openebs` and the Prometheus
   release were left behind. (A first attempt, runs/2026-09-27T183541Z_smoke-scripted-interrupted,
   killed the wrong process and ran normally; it tests nothing.) The next problem (runs/2026-09-27T184947Z_smoke-scripted-afterkill)
   recorded `removed_harness_services` [prometheus, openebs], deleted the namespace, and started with
   nothing preexisting and no drift.
5. **A planted object outside the reset's reach.** A config map in `observe` and a label on
   `kind-worker`: the next problem (runs/2026-09-27T185215Z_smoke-scripted-planted) was refused before
   the agent's first action with `cluster_drift` [changed: Node/kind-worker, new:
   observe/ConfigMap/planted-drift]. With both removed, `--resume` ran it `ok`, clean, with no
   environment change recorded, its reset also removing the services the refused attempt had left.

The baseline is committed with this note.
